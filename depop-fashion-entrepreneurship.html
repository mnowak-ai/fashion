A billion dollars of "small" sellers

In 2025, people sold over one billion dollars worth of clothes, shoes, and accessories on Depop. Here is the wild part: most of that money did not go to big companies. It went to regular people, including students, selling from their bedrooms.

Depop looks like a social media app. You scroll a feed, follow sellers you like, and tap to buy. But behind that feed are more than 3 million active sellers running real shops. They photograph items, write descriptions, set prices, answer customers, and ship packages. That is not a hobby. That is a business, and they built it with a phone.

Why this matters for students

Fashion used to be an industry you needed permission to enter. You needed a boutique to carry your stuff, a bank to loan you money, or a company to hire you. A "no" from any of them could end the dream before it started.

Resale apps flipped that. Today a student can find an underpriced jacket at a thrift store on Saturday, photograph it Saturday night, and sell it to a buyer across the country by Monday. Along the way they are learning pricing, marketing, photography, customer service, and profit math. Those are the exact same skills that run billion-dollar brands, just at a size that fits a school schedule.

And the market they are stepping into is not small. Depop's US sales grew about 60 percent in one year, and the platform was just acquired by eBay in a deal worth over a billion dollars. Secondhand fashion is not a trend on its way out. It is one of the fastest-growing corners of the entire industry.

You no longer need permission to start a fashion business. You need a phone, a plan, and your first item.
