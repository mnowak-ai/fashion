Why Pinterest sees trends first

Here is Pinterest's superpower in one word: planning. People do not go there to show off what they wore yesterday. They go there to plan what they want next: the prom outfit, the room makeover, the fall wardrobe. That means Pinterest hears about trends months before they show up in stores or on your feed.

Pinterest's AI reads billions of those searches and saved images, spots which ideas are quietly climbing, and surfaces them in a free tool called Pinterest Trends. Every year the company also publishes Pinterest Predicts, its official forecast of what is about to blow up. The receipts are wild: over the last six years, 88 percent of its predictions came true. Machine learning finds the patterns; human experts curate the final calls. Sound familiar? It is the exact AI-plus-judgment recipe from this whole series.

Now the fun part: turning trends into money

Remember Depop, the resale app where students run real shops from their phones? Here is where the two tools snap together like puzzle pieces. Pinterest tells you what people are about to want. Depop is where you sell it to them. Smart sellers check Pinterest Trends before a weekend of thrifting, hunt for pieces that match rising searches, and list them just as demand arrives. That is not luck. That is forecasting, the same skill trend professionals get paid for, practiced with a bus pass and ten dollars.
